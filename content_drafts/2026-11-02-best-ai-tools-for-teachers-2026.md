## NotebookLM: Free Research and Curriculum Assistant

NotebookLM is Google's free research tool that lets you upload your own PDFs, district documents, and lesson materials, then ask questions grounded only in those sources, with citations to the exact passage. For teachers, that means turning a 200-page curriculum guide into a searchable assistant, or uploading a textbook chapter and generating a study guide, quiz, and audio overview in minutes.

## Differentiation and Special Education Workload

Differentiation is where AI saves teachers the most time, because it is the most repetitive task in lesson planning. MagicSchool can take one reading assignment and produce versions at three reading levels, with adjusted questions and vocabulary lists, in a few minutes. Diffit does the same job with a free tier, generating leveled passages from any source text. NotebookLM can restructure your own materials into simpler summaries for students who need them.

For IEP support and special education paperwork, MagicSchool includes tools for goals, progress notes, and parent communication that follow the required formats. None of these tools write the final document for you, but they cut the drafting time from hours to minutes, which is the real win.

## The Safety and Privacy Rules for Classroom AI

Classroom AI comes with obligations that consumer AI does not. Student work is protected data, and a generic chatbot with vague retention policies is not an acceptable place to paste essays. The safe default in this guide: use tools built for schools (MagicSchool, Khanmigo), use NotebookLM with documents you control, and never upload student work with identifying information to a tool that does not state how it handles data.

Billing deserves the same caution. AI writing subscriptions are exactly the category where users report "unauthorized charges continuing years after cancellation, broken cancellation mechanism, vanishing credits." School purchases should go through approved procurement, and personal subscriptions should be reviewed every semester with a calendar reminder, because a forgotten $12 monthly tool is $288 over two years.
