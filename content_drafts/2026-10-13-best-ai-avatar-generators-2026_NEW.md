## How do you make an AI avatar video step by step?

The workflow is deceptively simple, and the quality gap between first-timers and regular users comes almost entirely from the script, not the tool. The standard pipeline across HeyGen and Synthesia is: pick or create an avatar, write or paste a script, choose a voice and language, select a scene template, and render. From the homepage promises alone — HeyGen's "AI videos starring you, made in minutes" and Synthesia's studio-quality output in 140+ languages — the same five steps apply to both.

Where experienced users differ:

- **They write for spoken delivery.** Short sentences, natural rhythm, no bullet-point density. Avatar platforms render the script verbatim, so prose written for reading produces stiff video.
- **They match avatar to content.** A training module wants a neutral, professional avatar; a marketing clip wants an energetic or friendly one. Synthesia's avatar lineup (including the newer Syren model) and HeyGen's cloned presenter avatars serve different briefs.
- **They plan scene changes.** A single static shot gets boring fast; breaking a five-minute script into scenes with different camera framing keeps retention up, and both platforms support scene-level editing.
- **They test lip-sync on one short clip before the full render.** A 30-second test costs a fraction of a full render and catches voice/avatar mismatch early — important because avatar minutes are the metered unit that makes long videos expensive.

The process is genuinely minutes for a simple clip, which is why the category replaced so much traditional talking-head production in 2026. The craft has moved to script and scene design rather than camera and lighting.

## What are the hidden costs of AI avatar tools?

The sticker price is the smallest part of the cost. Four costs hide in the fine print, and all of them scale with production volume:

- **Metered minutes.** Avatar platforms bill per generated minute, and every revision or re-render consumes minutes. A 10-minute training video with three rounds of edits costs far more than one render — the same "credits burn fast" economics as AI video generation generally.
- **Branding on free tiers.** Free output carries watermarks and limited resolution, which is fine for internal testing but unusable for client deliverables.
- **Avatar and voice add-ons.** Custom avatars, cloned voices, and premium voice packs are frequently priced separately from the base plan, so the "starter" price does not reflect the production-ready configuration.
- **Workflow integration.** Enterprise teams that need SSO, approvals, and API embedding usually land on enterprise tiers — Synthesia's "trusted by 90% of the Fortune 100" footprint is partly a consequence of this, not just a marketing claim.

The budgeting rule: estimate your monthly rendered minutes with a 3x revision multiplier, add custom avatar and voice costs, and compare that number against the plan, not the advertised entry price. The comparison with human production still favors AI for volume — but only if the plan actually covers your volume.
