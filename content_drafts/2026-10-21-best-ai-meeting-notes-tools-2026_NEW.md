# Best AI Meeting Notes Tools 2026: Fireflies vs Tactiq vs Otter

**Quick Answer:** Fireflies is the best all-round AI meeting notes tool for teams, with unlimited transcription and AI summaries on the free plan, 400 minutes of storage per team, and 100-plus language support, with Pro from $10 a seat a month. Tactiq is the best lightweight alternative, a free-forever Chrome extension with 60-plus languages and no stored recordings. Fathom gives Zoom users unlimited free recording and transcription, and Otter leads on raw transcription accuracy. The deciding factor is whether you need stored audio, which only the meeting-bot tools provide.

An AI meeting notes tool is software that joins or listens to your meetings, transcribes the conversation, and generates summaries, action items, and searchable transcripts automatically, and the 2026 market splits into two architectures. Tactiq is the browser-side school: a Chrome extension that adds live captions inside Google Meet, Zoom, and Teams, privacy-friendly and light, with no stored audio or video. Fireflies and Otter are the meeting-bot school: an assistant that joins the call, records it, stores the transcript and media, and builds a searchable team workspace. The architecture decides everything downstream, including price, privacy, and whether you can search last month's meeting, so this guide starts there, with free-plan details and pricing verified in October 2026.

## What Does Fireflies Actually Offer in 2026?

Fireflies is the most complete meeting notes tool in this comparison, and its free plan is generous enough to be the default recommendation. The [official pricing page](https://fireflies.ai/pricing), verified October 2026, confirms unlimited transcription with no meeting cap, unlimited AI summaries, the AskFred chat assistant, 400 minutes of meeting storage per team, 100-plus language support with automatic detection, Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams integration, plus mobile, desktop, and Chrome extension apps, and Fireflies' own plan breakdown on its [blog](https://fireflies.ai/blog/fireflies-pricing-which-plan-is-right-for-you/) walks through which tier fits which team. The free tier gives most teams several weeks of meetings before storage becomes a consideration. Paid plans start at Pro $10 per seat a month billed annually, with Business at $19 and Enterprise at $39, and Pro unlocks unlimited storage, conversation analytics, voice agents, and the 50-plus integrations the tool is known for. Fireflies also carries a 4.8 out of 5 rating and GDPR and SOC2 compliance marks, which matters for teams with sensitive conversations.

## Why Is Tactiq the Best Lightweight Choice?

Tactiq is the opposite architecture and the right pick when you want notes without a bot in your meeting. It is a Chrome extension that transcribes Google Meet, Zoom, and MS Teams in real time with speaker identification, supports 60-plus languages, and generates short or detailed AI summaries, and its homepage claims the tool is the number one transcription tool for Zoom and Meet, with 1 million plus meetings transcribed weekly, over 1 million users, and a 4.8 rating based on 3,000-plus Chrome Web Store reviews. The free plan is permanent, no credit card required, and paid plans start around $8 a month, which is the cheapest paid tier in this comparison. What you give up is storage: Tactiq does not record or store audio and video, so you can copy the transcript out but cannot search last month's meeting inside the tool. That is a feature for privacy-conscious users, since no recordings exist to leak, and it is the single strongest argument for Tactiq over Fireflies.

## How Does Otter Compare on Accuracy and Price?

Otter.ai is the transcription-accuracy leader in the category, and independent 2026 guides consistently rank it first for reliable transcription with strong collaboration features. Its free plan covers basic transcription, and Pro runs $16.99 per user a month, which is the most expensive entry price in this group, above Fireflies at $10 and Tactiq at $8. Otter has a mobile app with in-person recording capability, which neither Tactiq nor Fireflies offers, and it is the strongest pick for education use cases and live captions in classrooms. The trade is price and scope: you pay more for the same meeting summary job, and the accuracy premium matters most for non-English accents and dense technical jargon. If transcription accuracy is your bottleneck, Otter justifies the price; if you mostly need structure and search, Fireflies delivers more for less.

## Who Has the Most Generous Free Plan for Meeting Notes?

The free-plan winner depends on your meeting platform. Fireflies offers unlimited transcription and summaries with 400 minutes of storage, which is the most generous for teams using any platform. Fathom offers unlimited recording and transcription on the free tier with no storage cap, rated 5.0 out of 5 on G2, and it is the best free option specifically for Zoom users, with Team at $19 a user a month when you outgrow it. Tactiq's free tier is permanent and unlimited for live transcription through the extension, though it stores nothing. Krisp sits in its own lane, combining noise cancellation with transcription so you get clean transcripts even in noisy rooms, with no bot in your calls. The practical ranking: unlimited recorded storage favors Fathom on Zoom, unlimited everything favors Fireflies across platforms, and zero-storage privacy favors Tactiq.

## How Do Pricing and Limits Stack Up Across the Five Tools?

The price table is the fastest way to choose. Fireflies: free with unlimited transcription and 400 minutes storage, Pro $10 a seat a month, Business $19, Enterprise $39. Tactiq: free forever, paid from about $8 a month. Otter: free basic, Pro $16.99 a user a month. Fathom: free unlimited, Team $19 a user a month. Krisp: free tier with noise cancellation plus transcription, paid from a similar range. Language support splits too: Fireflies covers 100-plus languages on every plan, Tactiq 60-plus, Otter strong multi-language with live captions. Storage is the big differentiator: only Fireflies, Otter, and Fathom store recordings for later search, while Tactiq and Krisp work live and leave nothing behind. For most solo users the math is simple: Tactiq at $8 or free covers notes, Fireflies at $10 covers notes plus a searchable archive.

## Which Meeting Notes Tool Should You Choose in 2026?

Match the tool to your meeting volume and privacy needs. Solo user with occasional meetings, take Tactiq, it is free forever and installs in seconds. Team that wants a searchable archive and integration with your CRM, take Fireflies at $10 a seat, its 50-plus integrations and stored recordings make it the strongest team investment. Zoom-heavy user who wants unlimited everything free, take Fathom. Non-English accents or technical jargon where accuracy is the bottleneck, take Otter despite the price. Noisy home office and you want clean transcripts, take Krisp. The one mistake to avoid is buying a meeting-bot tool when you only need live notes, because you pay for storage and recording you will never use, and the privacy trade of storing every meeting is real even when the storage is convenient.

## What About Notta and Other Meeting Notes Tools?

The field has more players worth naming, and each covers a gap the top three miss. Notta is a strong alternative for non-English teams, with its own transcription engine and good support for languages like Japanese and Korean where American tools weaken, and it also records in-person meetings from its mobile app. tl;dv positions itself as a free-first AI note taker for video calls, useful when your budget is exactly zero and you want summaries without paying. Krisp combines noise cancellation with transcription, so its value shows up in noisy home offices rather than clean meeting rooms, and it is the pick when your transcript quality is ruined by background sound. Read AI and Circleback round out the enterprise side, with Read offering a free plan of about five meetings a month and Circleback adding mobile capture for in-person sessions. None of these displaces the top three, but the pattern matters: every specialized need, languages, in-person capture, noise, has a specialist, and the specialists are usually cheaper than the generalists.

## What Are the Privacy Considerations for Meeting Notes Tools?

Privacy is the hidden decision in this category, because these tools literally store what your team says. Fireflies and Otter keep recordings and transcripts on their servers, which enables search and AI summaries but creates a data trail that lives outside your control, and the compliance marks, GDPR and SOC2, are real but they describe processes, not protection from a breach. Tactiq and Krisp take the opposite route: no stored audio or video, with transcripts generated live in your browser, which means there is nothing to leak and nothing to subpoena, and that is the strongest argument for the browser school in regulated industries. The practical rules: ask which meetings may be recorded before enabling a bot, check whether transcripts contain confidential information like client names or compensation, and match the architecture to the sensitivity. A sales team discussing pricing can live with Fireflies; a legal or HR call should probably run through Tactiq or stay off the bots entirely.
