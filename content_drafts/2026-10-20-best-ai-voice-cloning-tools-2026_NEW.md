In 2026 the category splits into cloud services such as ElevenLabs, which trade convenience for a subscription, and open-source models such as Chatterbox, which trade setup time for a free MIT license. ## What Is Voice Cloning in 2026?

Voice cloning converts a sample of recorded speech into a reusable voice model. Two approaches dominate. Instant cloning, also called zero-shot or rapid cloning, works from a short sample, usually 5 to 30 seconds, and produces a usable voice in under a minute. Professional cloning trains a dedicated model on longer data, typically 10 to 30 minutes of clean, single-speaker audio, and returns higher fidelity that closely matches the original speaker's range and accent.

The regulatory context changed in 2026. Several states and jurisdictions now treat unconsented voice cloning of a real person as a distinct offense, and every major platform enforces some form of consent verification. ElevenLabs uses rights attestation on instant clones and Voice Captcha for professional clones, Fish Audio runs a live ownership check for pro clones, and Resemble requires verifiable consent from the voice talent. This matters when you compare tools, because the cheapest service is not always the one that will approve your professional clone.

### 1. ElevenLabs: The Quality Benchmark

ElevenLabs remains the benchmark for clone quality. The v4 model raised the bar again in 2026, and the platform supports 70 or more languages from a single cloned voice. Instant cloning works from about 10 seconds of audio on every paid plan, with the $6/mo Starter plan as the cheapest commercial entry point on [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/). Professional cloning needs a 30-minute minimum training set and unlocks higher fidelity.

The trade-off is price and usage. The free tier only allows preset voices with a 10,000 character monthly cap, so cloning itself is a paid feature. API pricing runs from $50 per million characters on Flash to $100 per million on the v3 tier, which makes long projects expensive. Independent tests also note that credits burn fast on longer projects, so track usage before committing to a big batch.

### 2. Fish Audio: Best Value Clone

Fish Audio is the cheapest serious clone on the market. The free plan already includes instant cloning, and the $15/mo Plus plan adds 250,000 credits, 10 private voice slots, and one professional clone slot, which undercuts ElevenLabs' $22 professional tier. The S2.1 Pro model handles short samples well and supports 83 languages, with the API billed at $15 per million bytes on [Fish Audio](https://fish.audio/).

The catch is polish and commercial terms. The interface assumes you know model versions and model names, and the free plan's commercial terms are contradictory, with the plan page saying yes and the FAQ saying no. Use Plus, not Free, for anything you publish.

### 3. Cartesia: Cheapest Paid Clone, Lowest Latency

Cartesia includes instant voice cloning on its $5/mo Pro plan, making it the cheapest paid cloning of any tool in this comparison, undercutting even ElevenLabs' $6 Starter. Sonic 3.6 and newer models use up to 60 seconds of audio to hold accents better, and the 40ms latency profile is built for real-time conversational agents, not narration.

What you give up is catalog and polish. Cartesia is a developer-first platform with a thinner ready-made voice library, and the $49 Startup plan is where professional clones begin.

### 4. Resemble AI: Cloning With Built-In Fraud Detection

Resemble AI is the only major platform that bundles voice cloning with deepfake detection. Rapid Clone works from 10 seconds of audio in under a minute, and Professional Clone trains on 10 to 25 minutes of data with explicit, verifiable consent from the voice talent. PerTh watermarking is available on every output, which makes it the pick for security teams that both make and detect synthetic voices.

The API requires a Business plan or higher, and the consumer subscription was retired in favor of pay-as-you-go pricing reported near $0.0005 per second in mid-2026. Its open-source Chatterbox model, covered below, is the free route into the same ecosystem.

### 5. Chatterbox: The Best Genuinely Free Clone

Chatterbox is an open-source voice cloning model from Resemble AI, released under the MIT license, which makes it free for personal, research, and commercial projects rather than a trial that expires. It clones a voice zero-shot from about 5 seconds of audio, and the official Hugging Face demo runs in the browser with no account or install.

The public demo is built for testing rather than batch production, and it is English-first, so multi-language work needs a different tool. For volume work, you can self-host it on your own hardware.

### 6. OpenVoice V2: Free Cross-Language Cloning

OpenVoice V2 is the free pick for non-English languages. It is MIT licensed, supports six native languages including Spanish, French, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean, and can read text in a language your sample never used. A single short clip is enough to clone a voice.

### 7. Hume and Notevibes: Emotion Control and All-in-One Narration

Hume Octave clones from as little as 15 seconds of audio, uploaded from a consenting speaker, and offers fine-grained emotion and delivery control on the $14/mo Creator plan. Notevibes takes the opposite route, bundling cloning into a complete narration pipeline where Noty turns a script into narration, a podcast, or a deck in your cloned voice from the $19/mo Creator tier, with cloning available from the $9 Starter.
