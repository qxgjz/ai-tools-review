## How We Tested AI Fun Tools in 2026

We judged each tool the way a normal user would, not as an enterprise buyer:

- **Free tier quality**: Can you get a genuinely useful result without paying?

## Best AI Cartoon and Avatar Tools

**ToonMe** turns photos into cartoon avatars and has been one of the most downloaded consumer AI apps, with versions on both the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/toonme-cartoon-maker/id6738602682) and Google Play. The free version produces watermark-free cartoon portraits from your photos, and premium unlocks more styles and higher resolution. Its App Store pricing runs from a few dollars weekly to roughly $29.99/year during promotions, typical of consumer avatar apps.

**MakeToon AI** (also sold as "Cartoon Yourself") is the main alternative for unlimited transformations. App Store listings show $4.99 per week or $29.99 per year for full premium access, with fair-use limits on how many transformations you can run. If you want one avatar for a profile picture, ToonMe's free tier is enough; if you are making a whole set of cartoon characters, MakeToon's annual plan is the cheaper route.

The genre rule in 2026: avatar apps monetize through short weekly subscriptions, so always switch to annual billing and cancel after one month if you only need a few images. Subscription traps are the biggest complaint across consumer AI apps, and credits that cap your output on lower tiers and do not roll over make monthly plans a bad deal for casual users.

## Are AI Fun Tools Safe for Kids and Photos?

Privacy is the real question with fun tools, because they ask for personal data (who you are buying for) and photos (for avatars).

- **Photo tools**: Never upload photos you are not comfortable having processed on a server. Avatar apps train or store uploads on their infrastructure unless their privacy policy says otherwise, and ToonMe's own app stores are the safest distribution channel. Read the privacy policy before uploading kids' photos.
- **Gift tools**: Gift Assistant explicitly states your data will not be stored, which is the right behavior, but not every gift tool is that careful. Prefer tools that process without accounts.
- **Game platforms**: Inworld and Convai voice features are designed for developers to control, but if you let kids talk to AI characters, supervise sessions, because character guardrails vary by implementation.

The general safety rule for fun AI tools: the most delightful tools are often the least careful with data, so check the privacy page before you hand over photos or personal details.

## The Hidden Cost of AI Fun Tools: Subscriptions to Avoid

The fun tools category has the worst subscription hygiene in consumer AI, and the pattern is consistent across apps. Weekly plans are priced to be forgotten: a $4.99 weekly avatar subscription quietly costs $260 a year, more than five times the annual plan. Credits that cap your output on lower tiers and do not roll over turn a $9.99 monthly plan into a recurring donation if you take a month off. And free trials that require a card convert into surprise charges for users who forget to cancel.

Our rules for buying AI fun tools:

- **Always pick annual billing** when the tool offers it. Avatar apps price annual plans at roughly one-tenth of weekly pricing, and the quality of the free tier rarely justifies paying at all.
- **Set a calendar reminder to cancel** before any trial ends. Do not rely on the app to remind you, because most do not.
- **Check the rollover policy** before buying credits. If unused credits expire monthly, your real cost depends on how consistently you use the tool, not on the sticker price.
- **Prefer no-account tools** for one-off jobs. Gift Assistant needs no account and stores nothing, which is the right model for a tool you use a few times a year.

These rules matter more than the features, because the same apps that delight on a Sunday afternoon bill like gym memberships. Choose annual, cancel promptly, and you will keep the fun without the financial hangover.
