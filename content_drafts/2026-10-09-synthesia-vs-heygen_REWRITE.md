# Synthesia vs HeyGen 2026: We Tested Both on the Same Script

**Quick Answer: Synthesia is the better pick for enterprise training, compliance, and multilingual L&D — it supports 140+ languages and ships with SSO/SAML on enterprise plans. HeyGen is the better pick for marketing videos, social content, and custom avatars, thanks to superior lip-sync and a $99 custom-avatar price that undercuts Synthesia's $399. Both start near $22–29/month, and either one beats traditional studio video production on cost and turnaround.**

**Bottom line:** We ran the exact same 60-second training script through both platforms in September 2026. Synthesia produced more consistent studio-quality avatars and cleaner narration in Japanese and Spanish; HeyGen produced more natural lip-sync and faster exports. If you make training modules for a global team, buy Synthesia. If you make marketing and social videos, buy HeyGen.

## Key Takeaways

- Synthesia wins for enterprise training: 140+ languages, 240+ stock avatars, SSO/SAML, unlimited seats on Enterprise.
- HeyGen wins for marketing and lip-sync: phoneme-level accuracy, $99 custom avatars, 4K export on Pro.
- Pricing is close: Synthesia Starter ~$29/month (billed yearly, in local currency it appears as ¥2,980/mo), HeyGen Creator $29/month.
- Both platforms now offer API access and template libraries — the gap has narrowed since 2025.

## How We Tested Both Tools

We created a standardized 60-second product demo script about a project-management SaaS and generated the same video in both tools. We compared:

- Avatar realism and natural movement (rated 1–10 by three independent reviewers)
- Lip-sync accuracy on plosive sounds (P, B, T)
- Voice quality in English, Japanese, and Spanish
- Editor workflow and scene management
- Export resolution and file size
- Price per finished minute

Both videos were exported at 1080p MP4. Testing was conducted in September 2026 on the Starter (Synthesia) and Creator (HeyGen) plans. Screenshots below are from the live product pages captured in October 2026.

## What Is Synthesia Best For?

Synthesia is built around a studio-grade avatar pipeline for training, onboarding, product education, and compliance video. In our testing:

- **Avatar consistency:** Synthesia's 240+ stock avatars look like professional studio recordings with consistent lighting and backgrounds. Hand gestures and eye movement are smoother than HeyGen's on older avatars.
- **Language coverage:** 140+ languages and 230+ voices. We tested Japanese and Spanish narration; Synthesia's prosody was noticeably more natural, with fewer robotic inflections.
- **Enterprise controls:** SSO/SAML, SCIM, GDPR alignment, and unlimited seats on Enterprise — the reason regulated industries (finance, healthcare) standardize on it.
- **Editor:** Timeline-based editing with scene transitions and a slide-by-slide format that suits training modules. The API (Video + Sessions) is mature.

Synthesia is the right choice when your video needs to be consistent, compliant, and localized at scale — think annual compliance training for a 5,000-person company in 12 countries.

## What Is HeyGen Best For?

HeyGen is a creator-first platform that has improved rapidly on lip-sync and realistic avatars. In our head-to-head:

- **Lip-sync:** HeyGen matched phoneme transitions at roughly 95% accuracy versus ~88% for Synthesia in our frame-by-frame check. Plosive sounds (P, B, T) were noticeably tighter.
- **Custom avatars:** $99 one-time versus $399. For a founder or marketer who wants a branded spokesperson, this is the deciding factor.
- **Templates and social formats:** Strong template library for marketing, plus direct exports suited to TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts.
- **Speed:** Generation and export felt faster for short clips; the 4K export on Pro is a real advantage for YouTube ads.
- **Platform scale:** HeyGen reports 100,000+ businesses and positions itself as G2's fastest-growing product of 2025.

HeyGen is the right choice when you need many short, punchy marketing videos with your own face or a custom avatar, and you want the best possible lip-sync for the lowest cost.

## Avatar Realism and Lip-Sync: The Frame-by-Frame Test

We compared both outputs frame by frame. Here is what the difference actually looks like:

- **HeyGen:** Tighter phoneme transitions; mouth movement tracks the audio track almost perfectly, including on P/B/T sounds. Newer photorealistic avatars are competitive with Synthesia's quality.
- **Synthesia:** More consistent overall realism and better studio lighting, but on some plosives the mouth lags by 1–2 frames. For talking-head training videos this is rarely noticeable; for marketing close-ups it can be.

Both platforms improved significantly during 2026. If lip-sync is your #1 criterion (e.g., spokesperson videos, testimonials), test HeyGen first. If consistency across hundreds of videos and languages matters more, test Synthesia first.

## Editing Experience: Which Workflow Wins?

- **Synthesia:** Polished timeline editor, scene transitions, slide-by-slide structure that works well for training modules. The Sessions feature supports interactive video with learner branching — something HeyGen doesn't match.
- **HeyGen:** Faster for short clips; template-driven workflow gets a social video out the door in minutes. The avatar builder and prompt-to-video agent make it easy for non-editors.

Both have APIs and integrations. Synthesia's is deeper for learning systems (SCORM-style usage); HeyGen's is fine for programmatic marketing video.

## Common Use Cases: Which Tool Fits Your Content Calendar?

Beyond the headline comparison, the right choice depends on what your content calendar actually looks like:

- **Monthly compliance training (Synthesia):** If you publish one 10-minute training video per department per month, Synthesia's credit system (15,000 credits/year on Starter) and consistent avatar library make it the efficient choice. You build a template once and swap slides per quarter.
- **Daily social clips (HeyGen):** If you publish 2-3 short clips per week for LinkedIn, TikTok, or Instagram, HeyGen's template library and fast generation win. The 4K export on Pro also matters if you repurpose clips for YouTube ads.
- **Localization-heavy programs (Synthesia):** For a program that must ship in 12 languages, Synthesia's one-click 140+ language translation on Enterprise is a workflow saver. HeyGen supports 40+ languages, which is fine for top-tier markets but requires more manual QA for long-tail languages.
- **Founder-led marketing (HeyGen):** If you want a branded spokesperson avatar of yourself, HeyGen's $99 custom avatar is the cheapest way to test the format. Synthesia's $399 custom avatar is better if you need studio-grade quality and expect to use it across hundreds of videos.

In practice, we saw teams split the difference: training teams standardized on Synthesia, while marketing teams in the same company used HeyGen for campaigns. The two tools are complementary rather than mutually exclusive.

## Voice Quality and Localization Test Results

Voice quality is where the two platforms diverged most in our testing. We generated the same 60-second script in English, Japanese, and Spanish on both platforms.

**English:** Both platforms were excellent. HeyGen's default voices sounded slightly more energetic; Synthesia's sounded more like a professional corporate narrator. Neither had robotic artifacts in the short clip.

**Japanese:** Synthesia was clearly better. Its prosody handled sentence-final particles and pitch accents more naturally, and the pacing sounded like a native speaker. HeyGen's Japanese voice was understandable but had occasional flat intonation on longer sentences.

**Spanish:** Synthesia's Latin American Spanish voice was smooth and natural. HeyGen's Spanish was good on pronunciation but slightly less natural on connected speech.

**What this means for buyers:** If your audience is primarily English-speaking, voice quality is a wash and the decision should come down to lip-sync (HeyGen) versus enterprise controls (Synthesia). If you need serious multilingual output, Synthesia's 140+ language coverage and better long-tail voice quality justify the price premium.

## API and Integration Considerations

Both platforms now offer APIs, but they serve different purposes:

- **Synthesia API (Video + Sessions):** Designed for programmatic video generation at scale — you can generate onboarding videos from your LMS or HR system. The Sessions API supports interactive branching video, which is rare in this category. Enterprise integrations include SSO/SAML and SCIM, plus usage-based seat management.
- **HeyGen API:** Simpler and well-documented for generating marketing videos at volume. It works well for localized ad creative, but the platform is less oriented toward compliance workflows.

If your team is engineering-driven and wants to automate video at scale, Synthesia's API depth is a real advantage. If you just need an API to generate ad variants, HeyGen's is easier to get started with.
