## What Is an AI 3D Generator and How Does It Work?

An AI 3D generator is a hosted or open-source service that uses a generative 3D foundation model to produce a polygon mesh — and often PBR textures, rigging, and animation — from a text description or one or more reference images. The pipeline is now standard across the category: you type a prompt or upload a sketch, the model reconstructs geometry in seconds to minutes, then you re-mesh, re-texture, auto-rig, and export to a game engine, slicer, or AR framework.

Three workflow patterns dominate the market in 2026:

1. **Text-to-3D**: type "low-poly knight with a glowing sword" and get a base mesh. Best for props, weapons, furniture, and recognizable objects. Meshy, Tripo, Rodin, and Sloyd all ship this.
2. **Image-to-3D**: upload a concept drawing, character sheet, or product render and the model rebuilds it as 3D geometry with the silhouette preserved. This is where Meshy and Rodin earn their reputation — a clean concept image becomes a textured model in roughly 2 to 4 minutes.
3. **Multiview-to-3D**: provide one main image plus up to three additional views to lock down proportions on complex objects (Meshy 7 supports this; Tripo's H3.1 does too).

The 2026 quality ceiling has moved decisively: Tripo released P2.0 with native quad meshes up to 25,000 quad faces, Meshy 7 shipped in August 2026 with stronger single-image geometry, and Rodin's 4K PBR textures are close to shippable for hero props. "AI 3D" no longer means "blobby placeholder" — it means production-tolerable assets, with cleanup still required on topology before real-time use.

## How to Evaluate an AI 3D Generator Before You Subscribe

Buying a 3D generator on the monthly price alone is the most common mistake in this category. The credit economy, licensing, and cleanup cost decide the real total. Before you commit, run this checklist:

1. **Count models per month, not credits.** Convert the plan's credits into usable textured models using the vendor's own docs (Tripo publishes ~55 credits/generation on high-detail; Meshy ~20-30 credits). A $20 plan that yields 50 models and a $20 plan that yields 120 models are not the same product.
2. **Check the free-tier license.** Meshy's free output is CC BY 4.0 (commercial use with attribution); Tripo's free output is public and non-commercial. If you need to ship something sellable for $0, the license matters as much as the credit cap.
3. **Verify export formats for your target.** Game engines want FBX/GLB; 3D printing wants watertight STL/3MF; AR wants USDZ/GLB. Meshy exports all of them; Tripo locks FBX behind higher tiers; Rodin is GLB/OBJ-focused.
4. **Test the cleanup cost.** Raw AI meshes need retopology or remeshing before production. Meshy's one-click remesh and Tripo's Smart Low-Poly reduce that cost to near zero; Rodin is watertight out of the box.
5. **Probe support quality.** Support is a real failure point across AI tooling — one reviewer of a competing AI service put it bluntly: "after you have bought it there's no one to answer your queries." Test the vendor's response time on a free-tier question before you pay.
