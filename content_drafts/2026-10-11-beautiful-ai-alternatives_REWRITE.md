## Is Gamma Better Than Beautiful.ai?

The two tools answer the same question differently. **Design control:** Beautiful.ai enforces auto-alignment and brand rules — your deck cannot look broken, but it also cannot look very different. Gamma generates varied, modern layouts and lets you restyle the whole deck in one click. **Speed:** both produce a first draft in under a minute; Beautiful.ai's strength is the final polish, Gamma's is the range of output. **Price:** Gamma's free tier competes with Beautiful.ai's 14-day trial, and its paid plans undercut the $14.50 Pro price for the same solo use. **PowerPoint:** Beautiful.ai imports and exports PPTX natively, which Gamma handles with more friction — a decisive detail for corporate teams whose reviewers live in Microsoft Office.

The honest verdict: if you present to clients and need guaranteed brand alignment, Beautiful.ai's $14.50 Pro is a fair price. If you need volume, variety, or a zero-cost start, [Gamma](https://gamma.app) wins the value equation, and [Canva](https://www.canva.com) covers the template-heavy middle ground. ## How to Pick the Right Presentation Tool for Your Team

The decision narrows to three questions. **Who builds the decks?** Non-designers do best with Canva or Gamma because the templates do the layout work; a designer on the team can extract more from Pitch or Beautiful.ai's rules. **Where do decks live?** If your company runs on Google Slides, Plus AI upgrades the workflow you already have; if it runs on PowerPoint, Beautiful.ai and Pitch keep PPTX round-tripping. **What is the budget?** At zero, Gamma and Canva's free tiers cover casual decks; at $15 a seat, Beautiful.ai's polish starts to pay for itself; above that, Pitch and Canva Teams scale better for bigger groups.

The r/SaaS founders' rule — *"AI tools actually worth paying for as an early-stage startup"* — applies here directly: subscribe to the tool whose output your team actually ships, and keep one free alternative for experiments. A 4-person team will spend $50-60 a month on one of these tools, and the one that earns its seat is the one whose decks go straight to clients without rework.


## Tome and Pitch: The Specialists Worth Knowing

**Tome** is not a slides tool in the traditional sense — it builds scrollable, narrative pages where each section flows into the next. For investor pitches, product narratives, and story-driven proposals, that format outperforms classic slides because the reader scrolls through your argument instead of clicking through it. The AI generates both the structure and the visuals, and the free tier covers real usage. The tradeoff: Tome decks do not feel like decks, which is exactly why teams that love it refuse to go back, and why teams that need a classic 16:9 deck for a conference stage should look elsewhere.

**Pitch** is the collaboration-first option: shared asset libraries, brand kits, and per-slide comments make it the choice for marketing and sales teams that iterate together. Its free tier is workable for small teams, and the paid plans undercut Beautiful.ai's $40 Teams price at comparable seat counts. The gap is AI depth — Pitch's generation is competent but less turnkey than Beautiful.ai's, so teams with heavy manual design needs may find Pitch is a better editor than a generator.

Neither specialist appears in every shortlist, but each solves a real problem the generalists miss: Tome for narrative, Pitch for collaboration. If your team's decks are really stories, or really group projects, those two deserve a trial ahead of the generic comparison.

## SlidesAI and Plus AI: The Conversion Tools

Two tools deserve a closer look because they change the workflow instead of the output. **SlidesAI** converts a text prompt or an outline into a complete Google Slides or PowerPoint deck in seconds, and its free tier handles short decks — the practical choice for lecturers and analysts who need slides from notes. **Plus AI** lives inside Google Slides and rewrites, expands, and designs your existing deck in place, which matters for teams with a library of established templates they want to keep. Both trade design freedom for speed and integration; neither replaces Beautiful.ai for brand-critical design work, but both remove the manual build step that the AI-first tools already eliminated.

The integration angle is the quiet differentiator across the whole category. Teams on Google Workspace should weigh Plus AI and SlidesAI first because the output lands in the tool they already use; teams on Microsoft 365 should weigh Beautiful.ai and Pitch for the same reason. ## The Workflow That Makes Any of These Tools Pay

Presentation AI pays off only when the deck survives contact with a real audience. The workflow that maximizes value on any of these tools is a three-stage loop: **draft with AI** — prompt the full outline and let the generator produce the first version; **edit for truth** — replace generated claims with your actual numbers, names, and dates, because the generator cannot know your business; **review on the screen you will present on** — project the deck, read it aloud, and fix the slide that breaks the flow. Teams that skip stage two publish decks with confident-sounding errors, and teams that skip stage three discover the layout problem in front of the client.

That loop is why the [what is an AI agent](/blog/what-is-an-ai-agent/) primer matters here: an agent that drafts the outline and collects the real data cuts the manual work further, and the [best AI agents 2026](/blog/best-ai-agents-2026/) ranking lists the tools that automate that pipeline. The presentation tool is the final renderer, not the whole workflow — whichever you pick, the discipline of editing for truth is what separates a polished deck from a polished mistake.
