# Best No-Code AI Tools 2026: Build Apps Without Coding

**Quick Answer:** For 2026, the best no-code AI tool depends on who will maintain the app. Bubble is the strongest for full-stack web and mobile apps — you generate with AI, then edit design, database, and logic visually without writing code, and its platform powers $16B in ecosystem GMV. Bolt is the fastest AI app builder for prototypes, generating polished, clickable apps from a single prompt with a genuinely generous 1M free tokens a month. GlideOS is the quickest spreadsheet-to-app path — upload a sheet, get a working business app. All three have usable free tiers, so the decision comes down to complexity and control.

A no-code AI tool is software that lets you build applications through visual editors and natural-language prompts instead of writing code. The 2026 generation of these tools does not just scaffold a landing page — it generates databases, backend logic, authentication, and mobile builds from a description, then hands you a visual editor to change any layer by hand. This guide compares the three tools that define the category — Bubble, Bolt, and Glide — with verified pricing, free-tier limits, and the tradeoffs reviewers actually hit in 2026.

## What Is a No-Code AI Tool?

A no-code AI tool combines AI app generation with a visual editor, so you can describe an app in plain language and receive a working application — pages, database schema, workflows, and privacy rules — without maintaining generated code. The distinction from an AI coding tool matters: tools like Bolt generate real code you can read and export, while platforms like Bubble keep every layer visual, so a non-developer can change logic and data directly.

The category exists because the bottleneck in 2026 is no longer ideas — it is implementation. A founder with a working prototype can validate demand before hiring developers, and an operations team can build internal tools without a ticket to engineering. The tradeoff shows up in the complaints: users report that "usage limits and token caps that break momentum mid-task come up again and again" on AI-first builders, and that some tools' output needs heavy correction because the AI "still hallucinates a lot... even on top models." The right tool minimizes both by keeping limits generous and the edit surface visual.

## How We Tested and Ranked the Best No-Code AI Tools

We evaluated the category on six criteria: AI generation quality, visual-editing freedom after generation, backend and database capability, free-tier generosity, pricing transparency, and code portability. We verified claims against official pricing and feature pages and against independent hands-on tests published in 2026 that ran the same booking-app build through multiple platforms.

Two patterns emerged. First, the AI-first builders (Bolt, Lovable, Emergent) win on speed but meter their AI usage in tokens or credits, and heavy revision loops consume those fast — one 2026 test noted a debugging loop on a large project can burn a million tokens in a couple of messages. Second, the visual platforms (Bubble, Glide) win on control and predictability: generation is free or cheap, and the editing you do after is not metered. That split — whether your edits are metered or free — is the single most useful lens for choosing.

## Bubble: Best for Full-Stack No-Code Apps

![Bubble homepage](/screenshots/real/webp/bubble-home.webp)

Bubble's homepage headline — "Build apps with AI, no code required. Launch apps, not prototypes" — captures its positioning: AI generation plus a visual editor that covers design, data, workflows, and privacy rules. The Bubble AI Agent (Agent 2) turns a plain-language description into a planned app with pages, a database, sample data, and workflows, and you can switch between the agent and the visual editor at any point. Bubble also ships a Mobile builder for iOS and Android and an MCP server so you can drive builds from Claude or Codex.

Bubble is the most production-proven platform in the category: the company reports $16B in ecosystem GMV, 4.6M+ users, and 5B page views per year, and publishes case studies like VoiceDrop, which reached seven-figure ARR in 12 months with 2,000+ customers, bootstrapped entirely on Bubble.

Pricing is transparent and structured around AI credits, as detailed in [Bubble's Agent 2 announcement](https://bubble.io/blog/agent-2/). The Free plan includes 500 AI credits per month; generating an app and the planning session do not consume credits, and working in the visual editor never does. Starter is $29/month billed annually ($32 month-to-month today, rising to $32 annual/$39 monthly on October 20, 2026) with 1,000 AI credits, a custom domain, and backend workflows. Growth is $119/month annual ($134 monthly) and Team $349/month annual ($399 monthly). The notable limitation: Bubble does not allow code export, so leaving the platform means rebuilding your app elsewhere.

## Bolt: Best No-Code AI Tool for Fast Prototypes

![Bolt homepage](/screenshots/real/webp/bolt-home.webp)

Bolt's homepage asks "What will you build today?" with a prompt box and one-click Website, Slides, App, and Prototype modes. Built by StackBlitz, Bolt runs entirely in the browser with zero setup, and it produces a clickable, surprisingly polished prototype faster than anything else in the category — in a 2026 side-by-side test it scored 8.6/10 overall and won on speed and ease of use ([redeepseek's no-code build test](https://redeepseek.io/blog/how-to-build-an-app-without-coding)). It generates apps in React, Vue, Svelte, Next.js, or Astro, and can produce real mobile apps through Expo, which competitors like Lovable cannot do.

Bolt's free plan is genuinely generous: 1M tokens per month (with daily caps), enough to build and iterate on small projects without paying. Pro starts at $25/month ($18/month billed annually) with 10M tokens and token rollover, and Teams is $30/member/month. The catch is token metering: tokens are consumed by the AI re-reading your codebase, so the same prompt gets more expensive as your project grows, and reviewers on Trustpilot complain about tokens burned on repeated fixes for the same issue — with some users spending most of a month's allowance on corrections alone. Bolt is also thinner on backend: it nudges you toward a separate Supabase instance, and authentication and data flows needed noticeably more prompting than Lovable in testing.

## Glide: Spreadsheet-to-App Without Code

![Glide homepage](/screenshots/real/webp/glide-home.webp)

Glide's homepage — "Turn spreadsheets into apps & agents you can trust to run your business" — describes the category's fastest path from data to app. GlideOS, the AI-native platform launched in August 2026, reads the structure and logic behind a Google Sheet, Excel file, or Airtable base, then builds the screens and workflows an operations team needs — field operations, inventory trackers, customer portals, and internal dashboards — with no template picking first. A Glide Agent handles AI work inside the app on a credit budget, and a database is included on paid plans.

GlideOS pricing starts free (one project with limited credits) and Solo is $25/month, per [Glide's pricing pages](https://www.glideapps.com/classic/pricing). It is a separate product from Glide Classic, which runs Explorer at $19-25/month, Maker at $49-60/month, and Business at $199-249/month depending on billing period. The strength is the learning curve: if your data already lives in a spreadsheet, you can have a mobile-ready app running in hours, and edits users make inside the app sync back to the source sheet. The limitation is data-model ceiling: complex permission logic and large external audiences will outgrow the spreadsheet paradigm.

## The Hidden Cost: Metered Generation vs. Free Editing

The pricing models of the three platforms fall into two camps, and the difference is the most practical filter for choosing. Bubble and Glide meter narrowly: Bubble charges AI credits only when the Agent builds or edits (app generation, planning, and all visual editing are free), and GlideOS charges credits for Agent work but leaves the builder itself usable. Bolt meters everything: every prompt consumes tokens because the AI re-reads your whole codebase to understand context, so the same request costs more as the project grows, and a correction loop on a large app can burn a million tokens in a couple of messages — the exact failure mode behind complaints that usage limits "break momentum mid-task."

For a solo founder building an MVP over a few weekends, Bolt's free 1M tokens are plenty, and the flat Pro tier is fine. For a team iterating daily on a real product, the metered-editing model of Bubble or Glide keeps the monthly bill predictable no matter how many changes you make. An independent six-platform test from 2026 reached the same conclusion: the AI-first tools win the first build, and the visual platforms win the hundredth edit ([emergent's no-code platform test](https://emergent.sh/learn/no-code-ai-platform)).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Bubble free to use?
Yes. Bubble's no-cost tier gives you the visual editor, AI app generation, and the Agent with 500 AI credits monthly. Generating an app and the planning session do not consume credits, and visual editing never does. You only need a paid plan to deploy to a custom domain, publish mobile apps, or add production capacity.

### How much does Bolt.new cost?
Bolt's free plan includes 1M tokens per month with daily caps. Pro starts at $25/month ($18/month billed annually) with 10M tokens and token rollover; Teams is $30/member/month. Larger projects typically need a paid plan because the AI re-reads your whole codebase per prompt, so token usage grows with project size.

### Can I build a mobile app with no-code AI tools?
Yes. Bubble builds for web, iOS, and Android from one editor with app store publishing, and Bolt generates Expo-powered React Native apps that publish to the App Store and Google Play. Glide produces responsive PWAs that work on mobile without an app store.

### What is the difference between Bubble and Bolt?
Bubble keeps every layer visual — design, database, workflows, and privacy rules — so non-developers can change anything by hand, and it does not allow code export. Bolt generates real code you can read and export to GitHub, runs in the browser, and is faster for prototypes, but its token-based pricing gets expensive on larger projects and its backend story is thinner.

### Are no-code AI tools secure enough for production?
The mature platforms are. Bubble is SOC 2 Type II compliant with SSO on Enterprise and built-in privacy rules; Replit ships SOC 2 and SSO; Glide's paid tiers add team controls. The tools that generate portable code (Bolt, Replit) let your engineering team review the output before production. Check the compliance posture of your specific plan before launching with real user data.

## Conclusion

The winner in 2026 matches the maintainer, not the demo. Bubble is the right choice when the app is a real product and a non-technical team needs to keep changing it — the most complete platform in the category, with generation that does not meter your edits. Bolt is the right choice when speed to a working prototype is everything and you have technical instincts to manage token spend. GlideOS is the right choice when your business already runs on a spreadsheet. All three have free tiers, so test your actual build on each before paying.
