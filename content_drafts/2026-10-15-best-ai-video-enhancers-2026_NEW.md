# Best AI Video Enhancers 2026: Upscale & Improve Quality

**Quick Answer:** For 2026, the best AI video enhancer depends on whether you process locally or in the cloud. Topaz Labs is the pick for anyone who needs maximum quality and local control — it is "the standard in image and video upscaling," with Emmy-winning technology, 2 billion images processed, and desktop apps that run entirely on your own machine. Runway is the best cloud pick because its paid tiers bundle unlimited 4K video upscaling with generation — Standard starts at $12/month billed annually with 625 credits, and Pro at $28/month includes unlimited 4K upscaling plus Topaz AI Upscale inside the same subscription. Kling is the budget option for turning AI-generated clips into higher-quality final renders, priced around $8.80/month, though its credit system is exactly where users report the category's worst burn rates. The decision rule: local tools (Topaz) cost one-time license money and protect your footage; cloud tools (Runway, Kling) charge per credit and are faster to start, but "usage limits and token caps that break momentum mid-task" bite hardest at production volume.

An AI video enhancer is software that uses machine learning models to improve video quality — upscaling low-resolution footage to 4K, increasing frame rates for slow motion, sharpening detail, reducing noise, and restoring compressed or AI-generated clips. The category split in 2026 into two workflows: local desktop tools that process video on your own GPU, and cloud platforms that meter enhancement by credits. The difference matters because video files are huge and models are expensive to run — an hour of 1080p footage can take hours of processing, and the billing model decides whether that processing is a one-time license or a per-minute subscription cost.

The reason Topaz wins on quality is control. Local processing means the full-resolution source stays on your machine, no upload queue, no per-minute credit countdown, and you can run the same clip repeatedly with different models until the result is right. That workflow is irreplaceable for commercial work: when a client's archive footage needs to reach 4K for a broadcast or print use case, you want the best model and unlimited attempts, not a metered one. The trade-off is hardware and time: you need a capable GPU, and high-resolution upscaling of long footage is slow. For a solo creator or a studio that already owns the hardware, the local workflow is the highest-quality and, at scale, the cheapest route — a one-time license instead of per-minute credits.

## Do AI video enhancers actually improve quality without artifacts?

The honest answer is that enhancement is a trade, not a free lunch. AI upscaling reconstructs detail that does not exist in the source, and every model makes a judgment about what that missing detail should look like — sometimes it adds welcome texture, sometimes it invents artifacts: oversharpened edges, waxy skin, morphing motion in complex scenes. Runway reviewers report occasional physics glitches where objects morph or melt, and the same caution applies to every enhancer: "it still hallucinates a lot" is a complaint users level at top models, and an upscaler hallucinating detail is the visual equivalent. The defenses are practical. First, compare the source and output side by side at 100% zoom before committing to a full render. Second, use enhancement-specific models for their intended job — a denoise model for noisy footage, an upscale model for resolution, a frame-interpolation model for slow motion — rather than one catch-all filter. Third, keep the source files: enhancement is destructive on the original resolution, and you may need to re-render with a newer model next year. Fourth, test on 10-20 seconds of your actual footage before processing the full clip, because your specific compression artifacts and motion patterns determine whether the model helps or hurts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Topaz Video AI worth the price?
If you process video professionally and own a capable GPU, yes — Topaz Video AI is a one-time license for unlimited local processing, which is dramatically cheaper than cloud credits at high volume, and its Emmy-winning upscaling is the quality benchmark. If you only enhance a few clips a month and have no GPU, a cloud plan like Runway Standard bundles 4K upscaling for $12-$15/month and is the cheaper entry. The license-versus-credits decision is purely volume math.

### Does Runway include video upscaling in its plans?
Yes — it is bundled rather than sold separately. The entry paid tier includes 4K upscaling and watermark-free exports, and the Pro tier adds unlimited 4K upscaling plus Topaz AI Upscale in the same subscription. Runway publishes the credit conversions on its pricing page, so you can calculate the exact cost of your upscaling volume before you subscribe.

### Why do AI video credits burn so fast?
Because every attempt — successful or not — spends credits, and the realistic keep rate is well below 100%. A single 5-second Gen-4.5 clip costs about 60 credits at 12 credits per second, over a tenth of Standard's monthly allowance, and failed generations are not refunded. The category's loudest complaint is credit burn at 30-40% failure rates on some platforms, so budget for roughly double your finished output.

### Can I upscale AI-generated video to 4K?
Yes, and in 2026 this is the category's fastest-growing use case. Topaz's Astra explicitly upscales video generated on Veo 3.1, Sora 2, Runway, and Higgsfield to 4K at up to 120fps, and Runway's 4K upscaling handles any uploaded video on paid plans. The reason creators do this is that AI generators produce compressed, detail-soft output by default — upscaling recovers the resolution and detail that the generator's output resolution loses, making the final render suitable for broadcast or print.

### What is the difference between local and cloud AI video enhancers?
Local tools like Topaz Video AI run models on your own GPU: one-time license cost, no upload, no metering, unlimited attempts, and the source footage never leaves your machine — the right choice for commercial work and privacy-sensitive footage. Cloud tools like Runway and Kling process on their infrastructure and bill per credit: faster to start, no hardware required, but every attempt costs money and your footage is uploaded to their servers. The choice is quality-and-privacy versus convenience-and-speed.
