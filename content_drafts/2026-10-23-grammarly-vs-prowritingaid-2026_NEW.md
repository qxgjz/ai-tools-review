# Grammarly vs ProWritingAid 2026: Which Editor Is Best?

**Quick Answer: Grammarly is the better choice in 2026 for everyday writing, meaning emails, docs, client work, and anywhere you type, because its free tier works all day and Pro costs $144 per year. ProWritingAid is the better choice for authors and long-form writers, because Premium costs less at $120 per year, offers a $399 lifetime license, and runs 25+ whole-document reports that catch pacing, repetition, and structure issues Grammarly never mentions. The decision is a stage-of-writing choice, not a quality contest: drafting and short-form work goes to Grammarly, manuscript editing and deep reports go to ProWritingAid.**

Grammarly is an AI writing assistant that checks grammar, spelling, tone, and clarity everywhere you type, from browsers to desktop apps to mobile, and it is used for quick professional editing across business and academic writing. ProWritingAid is a manuscript-analysis suite that combines line editing with 25+ whole-document reports, and it is used by novelists and long-form writers to strengthen structure, pacing, and storytelling. Both tools have been around for over a decade, but they serve different audiences with different strengths.

## What Is ProWritingAid and How Is It Different From Grammarly?

ProWritingAid is a writing analysis suite built for long-form work. Its core differentiator is whole-document reporting: instead of only flagging individual sentences, it reads the entire manuscript and produces 25+ reports covering pacing, echoes, sticky sentences, dialogue tags, sensory detail, and sentence-length plateaus. It also integrates with Scrivener and Vellum, the tools novelists actually use, and it offers a one-time lifetime purchase that Grammarly cannot match.

Grammarly, now part of Superhuman, is the everyday editor. It delivers instant inline corrections in 500,000+ apps and websites through its browser extension and desktop app, includes tone detection and full-sentence rewrites, and bundles plagiarism detection into the paid plan. Its free tier is genuinely useful, catching grammar, spelling, and punctuation all day with no word limits, which makes it the lowest-friction option for most professionals.

## What Does ProWritingAid Do That Grammarly Cannot?

ProWritingAid is the only one of the two that reports on the document as a whole. It flags sentence-length plateaus that make long passages monotonous, repeated echoes that tire readers, pacing dips across chapters, and overused dialogue tags in fiction. These reports are built for novelists: the dialogue tag report, the pacing analysis, and the sensory detail check have no equivalent in Grammarly.

The Scrivener and Vellum integrations are another structural advantage for book authors. A novelist drafting in Scrivener can run ProWritingAid reports without leaving the writing environment, and the tool's fiction-aware analysis is designed around story structure rather than business communication. The tool is also endorsed by working authors: Leeanna Morgan, a 59-novel author, says it bridges the gap between her knowledge as a reader and her ability as a writer, and Melle Amade reports the reports save her roughly $2,000 per manuscript in editorial costs.

Grammarly does not attempt this kind of analysis. Its suggestions are sentence-level, with a single overall score rather than a set of deep reports. For writers producing a book, a thesis, or any document where structure and repetition decide whether it reads well, that is a meaningful gap.

## How Do the AI Writing Features Compare in 2026?

Both tools added substantial generative AI in the last two years, but they use it differently. Grammarly's generative layer is built for everyday communication. Pro users get 2,000 AI prompts per month, full-sentence rewrites that preserve the original meaning, tone suggestions that adapt the message to context, and a humanizer agent that gives writing personality. The AI Detector agent spots AI-generated text and flags areas to revise, which is useful for teams checking AI-assisted drafts. These features work inline across the browser extension, desktop app, and mobile apps, so the AI assistance appears exactly where the writer is already typing.

ProWritingAid's AI is oriented around the manuscript. The tool offers unlimited rephrases on Premium, plus daily Sparks, which are contextual suggestions that appear while you write, with the count rising on higher tiers. Story Credits cover story-specific analysis for novelists, and the reports themselves are the core AI value: pacing analysis, dialogue tag checks, and sensory detail scans are driven by models that read the whole document. ProWritingAid is explicitly not designed to generate large sections of text, since its features are meant to inspire and refine rather than write for you, while Grammarly is comfortable producing email responses, outlines, and short drafts from a prompt.

The practical implication is simple. If your work involves generating new text, such as drafting replies, building outlines, or summarizing documents, Grammarly's 2,000 monthly prompts and inline rewrite tools cover it. If your work involves shaping existing long-form text, such as a novel chapter or a thesis section, ProWritingAid's reports and Sparks provide analysis no prompt-based tool offers. Writers who do both typically keep Grammarly for generation and ProWritingAid for structural passes.

## Why Is Grammarly Better for Everyday Writing?

Grammarly wins on speed, reach, and free-tier generosity. The free plan catches grammar, spelling, and punctuation without word limits, so a professional can use it all day without paying. Pro adds full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustment, plagiarism detection, and 2,000 AI prompts per month, plus team features such as style guides that were previously locked in the Business plan.

The integration footprint is the largest in the category. Grammarly works in 500,000+ apps and websites through the browser extension, plus dedicated desktop and mobile apps. Corrections appear inline while you type in Gmail, Slack, Google Docs, Word, and most web forms, which is exactly the experience professionals want for email and client work. The mobile app covers Android and iOS, where ProWritingAid has no mobile offering at all.

User sentiment backs this split. On the Chrome Web Store, ProWritingAid scores 4.8 against Grammarly's 4.5, but Grammarly's review volume is far larger and its accuracy carries the lowest false-positive rate among the major grammar checkers in recent testing. For most business and academic users who write in short bursts, Grammarly's instant, everywhere correction is worth more than deep reports they will never run.
