# Best AI Music Generators 2026: Suno vs Udio vs Soundraw

An AI music generator is a tool that turns text descriptions or audio references into complete songs, instrumental tracks, or beats, using generative models trained on music, and it is used by content creators, producers, and marketers to produce usable audio in minutes instead of days. The category in 2026 has split into prompt-to-song tools that output full songs with vocals, royalty-safe beat makers for monetized video, and composition tools for scoring.

**Bottom line up front:** **Suno** is the best AI music generator for most creators in 2026, with a genuinely usable free tier (50 credits a day), the fastest prompt-to-song pipeline, and paid plans from $10/month (2,500 credits) that include commercial rights. Choose **Udio** (Standard from $10/month, 2,400 credits) when you want deeper genre and vocal control. Choose **Soundraw** when you need 100% copyright-safe beats for monetized videos and ads, and **ElevenLabs Music** when you want music generation inside a broader voice and audio platform. Budget $10 to $30 per month; the real cost driver is credits, because one finished song usually takes several generations.

## Suno: The Fastest Prompt-to-Song Pipeline

**Definition:** Suno is an AI music generator that creates complete songs, including vocals, instruments, structure, and mastering, from a plain text description, and it is used by hobbyists and working producers to go from idea to finished track in under a minute.

![Suno official homepage](/screenshots/real/webp/suno-music-creator.webp)
*Suno's official site (captured October 2026) markets "Make any song you can imagine," with a chat-to-make-music input and media logos including Rolling Stone and Billboard.*

Suno remains the category leader for one reason: speed and completeness. A single prompt can return two full song variations with vocals in under a minute, and the free tier's **50 credits per day** are enough to test the product seriously without paying. Paid **Pro is $10/month** (about $8/month on annual billing, per [Suno's official site](https://suno.com)) for 2,500 credits, roughly 500 songs, 20 downloads a month, and commercial use rights; **Premier is $30/month** (about $24/month annually) for 10,000 credits, 60 downloads, Suno Studio editing, and priority generation.

The catch is the credit model. Suno charges credits per generation, and re-rolling verses, swapping instruments, and extending sections all burn credits, so one released song typically costs several generations. Suno itself states that "neither generation credits nor download slots roll over," which is exactly the complaint users raise across AI tools: "credits cap your output on lower tiers and do not roll over." If you produce music daily, budget for Premier or buy add-on downloads once you hit the cap.

## Udio: Deeper Genre and Vocal Control

**Definition:** Udio is an AI music generator built around the same prompt-to-song idea as Suno, but with deeper genre control, a distinct vocal character, and a credit system that spends one credit per finished clip.

![Udio official homepage](/screenshots/real/webp/udio-home.webp)
*Udio's official site (captured October 2026) shows the Beta app's "Make your music. Create any song. Just describe it." prompt interface with example song cards.*

Udio's edge is musical control. Its models are better at genre fidelity and vocal texture, which makes it the pick for producers who want a specific sound rather than a generic pop result. Udio spends credits per finished clip: a 32-second song costs one credit, and you extend in sections, which can make full songs cheaper or more expensive than Suno depending on how you work. The free tier gives **10 credits a day with a 100-credit monthly cap**; **Standard is $10/month** (about $8/month annually) for 2,400 credits, and **Pro is $30/month** (about $24/month annually) for 6,000 credits, per current plans published on [Udio's announcements page](https://www.udio.com/announcements). Annual subscriptions receive a 20% discount, and credits do not carry over between months.

The practical takeaway: Udio rewards patience. If you are willing to iterate on prompts and sections, you can get closer to a specific artistic vision than Suno, at roughly the same cost. If your bottleneck is speed and volume, Suno stays ahead.

## ElevenLabs Music: Music Inside a Voice and Audio Platform

**Definition:** ElevenLabs Music is the music generation feature inside the ElevenLabs audio platform, which is best known for text-to-speech and voice cloning, letting teams generate music alongside voiceover in one subscription.

If your workflow already uses ElevenLabs for voiceover, its music feature removes a second subscription. The platform's free tier includes monthly credits, and paid plans start at $6/month (Starter) with the Creator plan at $22/month, verified on [ElevenLabs' official pricing page](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing). Music generation quality is strong on melodic and instrumental output, though it does not yet match Suno on full-song vocal production. The advantage is consolidation: generate the voice track and the backing music in the same system, with the same credit pool.

## Soundraw: Copyright-Safe Beats for Monetized Content

**Definition:** Soundraw is a royalty-safe AI beat-making platform whose model is trained exclusively on in-house music, so every generated loop is legally yours to monetize without sampling claims.

Soundraw is the answer to the licensing question that trips up every other tool. Its catalog is generated from proprietary music, which means you can use the output in monetized YouTube videos, ads, and streams without worrying about copyright claims or sampling. The workflow is mood, genre, and tempo matching rather than text-to-song, which makes it less creative but far more predictable for background music. The free tier is limited, and paid Creator plans unlock unlimited downloads of the generated library. If your job is "video needs a clean beat that will never get claimed," Soundraw is the safest pick in this comparison.

## AIVA, Beatoven.ai, Mubert, and Stable Audio: Niche Picks

**AIVA** is the scoring tool: it composes original music in styles from orchestral to electronic, with composer-level control, and is a favorite for film and game trailers. **Beatoven.ai** generates no-copyright background music from mood and duration, a strong low-cost option for podcast and tutorial audio. **Mubert** runs a generative streaming model that produces endless music and offers an API for apps and streams. **Stable Audio** by Stability AI takes text prompts to audio and is the experimental pick for sound designers. None of these four beat Suno or Udio on full-song output, but each wins a specific niche: scoring, background clearance, live streams, and sound design.

## Which AI Music Generator Should You Choose?

The choice comes down to three questions. First, what are you making? Full songs with vocals point to Suno or Udio; background beats for monetized video point to Soundraw; scoring and trailers point to AIVA. Second, how much do you generate? If you produce daily, a subscription with a credit pool that matches your volume beats a pay-per-generation tool, and remember that no tool here rolls credits over, so the monthly volume, not the monthly peak, should set your tier. Third, where does the music live? If your workflow already runs on ElevenLabs for voiceover, its music feature may be cheaper than a second subscription. Answer those three questions and the right tool names itself.

## Why Do AI Music Generators Burn Through Credits So Fast?

The #1 user complaint in 2026 is credit burn. A single finished song is rarely a single generation: you re-roll the first verse, extend the chorus, swap the instrumentation, and regenerate the mix, and each step costs credits. On lower tiers, the monthly reset means a heavy production week can leave you blocked mid-project. The same dynamic is documented for video in our [AI video credits deep dive](/blog/ai-video-generator-credits-burn-fast-2026/), and the math is identical: one usable output equals three to five generations. The practical fixes are to compose in sections instead of full songs on free tiers, batch your idea generation and only refine winners, or buy the tier whose credit count matches your weekly volume. Users also report that "purchased credits and now it just times out" when a tool's queue is overloaded, so a priority-queue plan is worth the upgrade if you generate at peak hours.

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the best AI music generator in 2026?**
Suno is the best overall for most creators, with the fastest prompt-to-song pipeline and a usable free tier. Udio is better when you need specific genre and vocal control, and Soundraw is the pick when copyright-safe beats matter more than creativity.

**Q2: Can I use AI-generated music commercially?**
Yes, on paid tiers. Suno Pro and Premier, Udio Standard and Pro, and Soundraw's paid plans all include commercial rights. Free tiers are typically limited to non-commercial use, so check the license before monetizing.

**Q3: How much does an AI music generator cost per month?**
$10 to $30 per month for the main players. Suno Pro is $10/month for 2,500 credits, Premier is $30/month for 10,000 credits; Udio Standard is $10/month and Pro is $30/month. Annual billing cuts about 20%.

**Q4: Which AI music generator is best for YouTube videos?**
Soundraw for guaranteed copyright-safe background music, or Suno on a paid tier for full songs you own. For short background tracks, Beatoven.ai is a low-cost alternative.

**Q5: Do AI music credits roll over?**
No. Suno and Udio both state that generation credits and download slots do not roll over between months, which is why heavy users should size their tier to monthly volume rather than monthly peaks.

**Q6: Which tool has the best vocal quality?**
Suno produces the most convincing full-song vocals out of the box. Udio gives you more control over vocal texture, and ElevenLabs' voice platform is best when you need a specific cloned voice on a track.

Suno remains the default pick for most creators, Udio wins for control, and Soundraw is the safe choice for monetized video. Budget credits before you budget money, because every tool meters output, and one finished song usually costs several generations.
