<h3>What Could Be Better</h3><ul>
<li>Additional cost on top of Notion subscription — Notion AI is an add-on that costs $10/month per member (or $8/month per member if billed annually), in addition to your existing Notion subscription. While Notion AI is included for free in some higher-tier plans (Notion Business and Enterprise include a certain number of AI responses per month), most users on the Free or Plus plans need to pay extra for AI. This means the total cost for a Notion user who wants AI is: Notion Plus ($8/month) + Notion AI ($10/month) = $18/month per user. For teams, this can add up quickly — a team of 5 would pay $90/month for Notion Plus + AI, and a team of 10 would pay $180/month. comparison this to: ChatGPT Plus ($20/month, includes GPT-4 and DALL-E, no per-user team pricing on the consumer plan), Claude Pro ($20/month), or Jasper ($39/month per user, but includes more specialized AI writing features). While $10/month for AI is reasonable, the fact that it's an add-on on top of an already paid Notion subscription can feel expensive, especially for individual users or small teams. Notion does offer a free trial of AI (200 AI responses), and there's a pay-as-you-go option for occasional users, but for regular use, the subscription is required. For budget-conscious users, the additional cost may be a barrier — they might opt to use a free or cheaper AI tool (like the free version of ChatGPT) alongside Notion instead of paying for Notion AI.</li>
<li>AI writing less specialized than dedicated tools — While Notion AI is a capable general-purpose AI assistant, its writing capabilities are less specialized and less powerful than dedicated AI writing tools like Jasper, Copy.ai, or Claude. Notion AI is designed as a general productivity assistant — it can write, edit, summarize, and brainstorm, but it doesn't have the specialized features and templates that dedicated writing tools offer: no marketing-specific templates (ad copy generators, landing page copy, email sequences — though you can use custom prompts), no SEO optimization features (keyword analysis, SEO scoring, content optimization — though Notion has some SEO features for published pages), no brand voice customization (you can't train the AI on your brand's specific voice and style), no plagiarism detection, no content performance analytics, and no specialized workflows (like Jasper's campaign workflows or Copy.ai's project-based organization). I found that for general writing (notes, documents, essays, basic blog posts), Notion AI was excellent. But for specialized marketing content (ad copy, landing pages, email sequences, SEO-optimized articles), I got better results from Jasper or Copy.ai, which have specialized templates and features for those use cases. comparison this to: Jasper (specialized for marketing content with 50+ templates, SEO integration, brand voice), Copy.ai (specialized for marketing copy with workflow-based organization), or Claude (excellent long-form writing with nuance and depth). For users who primarily need AI for general productivity and document creation within Notion, Notion AI is sufficient. For users who need specialized marketing or SEO writing features, a dedicated writing tool may be better.</li>
<li>Limited to Notion ecosystem (no external use) — Notion AI is limited to the Notion ecosystem — you can only use it within Notion (web app, desktop app, mobile app). There's no browser extension (so you can't use Notion AI on other websites like Gmail, Google Docs, or WordPress), no standalone AI app, no API for external use (you can't integrate Notion AI into your own applications or workflows outside of Notion), and no integration with third-party tools (though Notion itself has many integrations, the AI features are not exposed through those integrations). This means if you want to use AI for tasks outside of Notion (e.g., writing an email in Gmail, drafting a document in Google Docs, writing a blog post in WordPress, responding to messages in Slack), you can't use Notion AI — you'd need a separate AI tool with a browser extension or API. I found this limiting: I often wanted to use Notion AI's writing and editing capabilities in other apps (especially Gmail for emails and Google Docs for shared documents), but I had to switch to ChatGPT or another tool instead. comparison this to: ChatGPT (has a browser extension, API, and integrations with many apps), Jasper (has a Chrome extension, API, and integrations), or Claude (has a browser extension, API, and integrations). For users who want an AI assistant they can use across multiple apps and platforms, Notion AI's ecosystem lock-in is a significant limitation. You'd need to use Notion AI for Notion tasks and a separate AI tool for everything else, which adds cost and complexity.</li>
<li>Limited customization and advanced settings — Notion AI offers limited customization options and advanced settings compared to more specialized AI tools. You can't: fine-tune the AI model on your own data or content (to make it more aligned with your specific use case or industry), create custom AI templates or workflows (you can use custom prompts, but you can't save them as reusable templates with specific settings — though Notion's database templates can include AI blocks), adjust advanced AI settings (temperature, top_p, max tokens, model selection — Notion AI uses a fixed model and settings, though they may use different models for different tasks), create custom AI commands (you can't add your own '/' commands for frequently used prompts — though you can use custom prompts manually), or set up AI automations with complex logic (Notion's automation features are basic compared to tools like Zapier or Make, and the AI integration in automations is limited). I found that for basic use, the default settings were fine. But for more advanced use cases (e.g., training the AI on my company's specific writing style, creating reusable AI workflows for my team, or setting up complex AI-powered automations), the limited customization was frustrating. comparison this to: Jasper (brand voice customization, custom templates, workflow automation), ChatGPT (custom instructions, GPTs, API with full parameter control), or Claude (custom system prompts, API with parameter control). For power users who want to customize and fine-tune their AI experience, Notion AI's limited customization may be a drawback. For casual users who just want basic AI features, the default settings are sufficient.</li>
<li>Q&A and advanced features limited to expensive plans — Some of Notion AI's most powerful features, particularly Q&A (asking questions across your entire Notion workspace and getting AI-generated answers based on your content), are limited to the most expensive plans. Notion AI Q&A, which lets you ask questions like 'What were the key decisions from last quarter's planning meetings?' or 'What are the action items from the Q3 project reviews?' and get answers based on the content in your Notion workspace, is only available on Notion Business ($15/month per user) and Enterprise (custom pricing) plans, not on the Plus ($8/month) plan. For users who want accurate, reliable AI output, it's important to remember that Notion AI, like all AI tools, requires human oversight and fact-checking. Notion does include some safeguards (like citing sources for Q&A answers), but the fundamental limitation of AI hallucination remains.</li>
</ul>
