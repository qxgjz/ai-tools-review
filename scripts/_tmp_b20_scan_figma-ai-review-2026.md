The generated components followed the client's design system (specific colors, fonts, spacing) and were properly structured with variants and auto-layout. <h3>What Could Be Better</h3><ul>
<li>Only available on expensive Organization/Enterprise plans — Figma AI is currently only available on the Organization plan ($45 per editor/month) and Enterprise plan ($75 per editor/month). This is expensive for individual designers, freelancers, or small teams. The Professional plan ($15/month), which is what most individual designers and small teams use, does not include Figma AI features. comparison this to: Adobe Firefly (included in Creative Cloud $54.99/month, which many designers already have, or available as a standalone $4.99/month), Midjourney ($10/month basic plan), or Canva AI (included in Canva Pro $12.99/month). For individual designers who want to use AI design features, the cost of upgrading to Figma Organization ($45/month vs $15/month = $30/month extra) may be hard to justify, especially since there are cheaper alternatives. Figma has said they're working on bringing AI features to lower tiers, but as of my testing, only Organization and Enterprise users have access. This pricing strategy excludes a large portion of Figma's user base.</li>
<li>AI-generated designs can be generic — While Figma AI's layout generation is useful, the generated designs can be generic and formulaic. The AI tends to follow common design patterns and trends without much originality or brand-specific creativity. I generated 15 different UI layouts, and while they were all clean and functional, many looked similar — same card styles, same button placements, same navigation patterns. For a client project that needed a unique, brand-specific design, the AI-generated layouts were too generic and required significant customization to match the brand's identity. The AI also doesn't understand complex brand guidelines — it can follow basic colors and fonts, but it doesn't capture the subtle brand personality, visual language, or design principles that make a brand unique. For truly original, brand-specific designs, you still need a human designer's creativity and judgment. Figma AI is best used as a starting point or for generating common UI patterns, not as a replacement for creative design work.</li>
<li>Limited to UI/UX design (no illustration or photo editing) — Figma AI is focused exclusively on UI/UX design. It doesn't help with: illustration (generating or editing illustrations), photo editing (retouching, compositing, color grading), graphic design (posters, logos, branding), 3D design, or other design disciplines. If you're a multi-disciplinary designer who works on UI/UX, illustration, and photo editing, Figma AI only covers one part of your workflow. comparison this to: Adobe Firefly (integrated into Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign, and other Adobe tools, covering photo editing, illustration, and graphic design), Canva AI (covers social media graphics, presentations, posters, and more), or Midjourney (generates illustrations and concept art). For UI/UX specialists, Figma AI's focus is fine. But for designers who work across multiple disciplines, you'll need additional AI tools for non-UI work. Figma AI's narrow focus means it's not a comprehensive AI design solution for all designers.</li>
<li>Can make mistakes and doesn't always follow design systems — Figma AI, like all AI tools, can make mistakes. The generated layouts sometimes have: incorrect spacing (elements too close or too far apart), misaligned elements, inconsistent typography (different font sizes or weights than specified), colors that don't match the design system, or components that aren't properly structured. I found that about 20-30% of AI-generated layouts needed significant correction to meet my quality standards. The AI also doesn't always follow your design system — it may use colors, fonts, or components that aren't in your system, or it may not use existing components when it should. You need to review and correct AI-generated designs carefully, which adds time. For designers who expect the AI to generate perfect, production-ready designs, this can be frustrating. Figma AI is best used as an assistant that generates drafts you then review and refine, not as an autonomous design generator.</li>
<li>Learning curve for advanced AI features — While Figma AI's basic features (text-to-UI, copy generation) are easy to use, the advanced features (design system generation, accessibility analysis, component suggestions) have a learning curve. I spent about 2-3 hours learning how to use all the AI features effectively, and I'm an experienced Figma user. The AI features are spread across different parts of the Figma interface (some are in the right sidebar, some are in the menu, some are context-menu options), which can be confusing. The documentation and tutorials are decent but not comprehensive — some features aren't well-explained, and I had to experiment to figure out a few things. For less experienced Figma users or designers who don't have time to learn, the advanced AI features may go unused. Figma could improve by consolidating the AI features into a single, easy-to-find panel and providing better tutorials. If you just want basic text-to-UI and copy generation, the learning curve is minimal. But to get the full value from Figma AI, you need to invest time in learning.</li>
<li>Privacy and data usage concerns — Figma AI uses your designs and content to train and improve its AI models (unless you opt out on Enterprise plans). This raises privacy and data usage concerns, especially for designers who work on confidential or proprietary projects. If you're designing a new product for a client that hasn't been announced yet, the last thing you want is for your design data to be used to train an AI model that competitors could use. Figma does offer opt-out options for Enterprise customers, but Organization customers (the lowest tier with AI access) don't have the same level of control. comparison this to: Adobe Firefly (trained on licensed Adobe Stock content and public domain content, not on user designs without permission), or self-hosted AI tools (you control your data). For designers who work on sensitive or confidential projects, the data usage concerns may be a barrier to using Figma AI. Figma has updated its privacy policies to be more transparent, but the fundamental issue of using user data for AI training remains a concern for many designers.</li>
</ul>
